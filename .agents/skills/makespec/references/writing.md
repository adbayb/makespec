# Writing ADRs and RFCs

Apply these rules to record content and planning prose. Preserve technical meaning, required headings, IDs, comparison labels, and MUST/SHOULD/MAY priorities. Do not apply prose-style rules to code or quoted source material.

## Rules

- Avoid em dashes. Use commas, periods, colons, or parentheses.
- Lead with the problem, decision, or required behavior. Cut announcement phrases such as "Here's why" and "In this section, we'll".
- Remove filler, redundant qualifiers, and empty emphasis such as "It's worth noting" or "really". Keep qualifiers that change requirement scope or behavior, such as "only" and "by default".
- Name the responsible actor or component and prefer active voice. Write "The CLI rejects invalid IDs" rather than "Invalid IDs are handled".
- Make claims specific. Replace vague claims about importance or performance with concrete consequences, measurable requirements, or cited evidence. Mark uncertainty instead of inventing facts.
- Use plain technical language. Remove business jargon, sales language, and metaphors that obscure the behavior or tradeoff.
- Avoid rhetorical questions, dramatic fragments, formulaic "not X, but Y" contrasts, and forced three-item lists. Keep genuine comparisons and the options needed to evaluate a decision.
- Vary sentence length and paragraph structure without padding. Cut slogans, punchy closing lines, and sentences that repeat the preceding point.
